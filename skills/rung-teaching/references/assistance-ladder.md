# Assistance Ladder

Use the lowest rung that restores productive progress, then return responsibility to the learner.

## Level 0 — Independent attempt

No hint yet. Let the learner try.

## Level 1 — Diagnostic question

Ask a question that tests the relevant decision or principle.

## Level 2 — Attention cue

Point toward the relevant feature without explaining the solution.

## Level 3 — Recall cue

Prompt retrieval of a principle, rule, or earlier lesson the learner has already encountered.

## Level 4 — Narrow choice or partial structure

Reduce the search space without making the important judgment for the learner. Require justification of any choice.

## Level 5 — Explain the missing prerequisite concept

Teach the minimum concept needed when the learner lacks information required for productive reasoning. Then immediately ask them to apply it.

## Level 6 — Worked analogous example

Demonstrate the principle on a different example, then return to the learner's problem or a parallel one.

## Level 7 — Partial solution to the current problem

Show only the portion needed to unblock progress. Require the learner to complete the reasoning.

## Level 8 — Full solution

Use when the current instance has become a poor learning vehicle, continued struggle is no longer informative, or direct instruction is required by safety/consequence.

A full solution does not establish mastery. When appropriate, follow with:

1. learner explanation in their own words;
2. an unassisted similar problem;
3. a changed transfer problem.

## Escalation rule

Escalate when the learner is no longer making productive progress at the current rung.

Productive struggle usually means the learner has enough prerequisite knowledge to form plausible approaches, makes interpretable errors, responds to targeted cues, and is still learning from the attempt.

Unproductive struggle usually means random guessing, absent prerequisites, repeated mechanical moves without understanding, inability to interpret feedback, or effort spent on mechanics unrelated to the target skill.

## Fading rule

Assistance should decrease as competence rises:

```text
teacher carries reasoning
→ shared reasoning
→ learner carries reasoning
→ teacher audits
→ independence
```

Do not keep providing Level 5 explanations to a learner who can now succeed with Level 1 cues.

## Direct instruction is appropriate when

- prerequisite knowledge is missing;
- the learner has attempted the problem and continued struggle is not informative;
- a misconception is inefficient to correct through questioning alone;
- safety or high consequence makes trial-and-error inappropriate;
- the needed concept is primarily factual;
- a worked model is needed before the learner can recognize the task structure.

After direct instruction use:

```text
explain
→ learner restates
→ learner applies
→ teacher fades
→ learner transfers
```
