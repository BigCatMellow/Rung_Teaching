# Teaching Loop

Use this reference for normal Rung teaching interactions.

```text
ORIENT
→ ATTEMPT
→ DIAGNOSE
→ EXPLAIN
→ MINIMUM HELP
→ REATTEMPT
→ VERIFY
→ TRANSFER
→ RECORD LESSON
```

## Orient

State or infer what skill is being practiced and what success looks like. Give enough context for a meaningful attempt, but do not front-load every relevant fact.

## Attempt

Have the learner act before receiving unnecessary help. The attempt is both practice and evidence about where reasoning breaks.

## Diagnose

Do not immediately repair the work. Ask one specific diagnostic question that locates the failure.

Use:

```text
question
→ learner answer
→ test answer
→ next question
```

Avoid stacking several major questions into one response.

## Explain

Ask the learner to make important reasoning explicit. Useful forms include:

- Why does that method apply here?
- What evidence supports that conclusion?
- What would have to change for your answer to change?
- Which principle are you using?
- Why would the competing approach fail?

## Minimum help

Move only as far up the Assistance Ladder as needed to restore productive reasoning.

The question is not “Can I give the answer?” but “What is the smallest intervention that lets the learner resume the important thinking?”

## Reattempt

The learner changes the work. Hearing a correction is not the same as producing a correction.

## Verify

Check the revision against the criterion that failed. Do not accept “seems better” or confidence alone when a concrete test exists.

## Transfer

Change the example enough that imitation is insufficient. Vary context, surface details, order, competing methods, missing information, or plausible traps while preserving the underlying principle.

## Record lesson

When a correction appears reusable, treat it first as a candidate lesson:

```text
OBSERVATION
→ POSSIBLE PATTERN
→ TEST
→ REPEATED EVIDENCE
→ STANDING PRINCIPLE
```

Do not turn every one-off correction into a permanent rule.

## Steering check

At meaningful checkpoints determine whether to:

- CONTINUE
- CHANGE APPROACH
- NARROW SCOPE
- TEACH PREREQUISITE
- RESEARCH
- TEST MASTERY
- STOP

A locally successful exercise can still be practicing the wrong thing.
