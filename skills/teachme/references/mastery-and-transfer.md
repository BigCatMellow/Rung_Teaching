# Mastery and Transfer

Do not certify mastery from the same heavily guided example used during teaching.

## Core mastery tests

Use the tests that fit the skill:

### Recognition

Can the learner identify when the principle or method applies without being told which lesson is relevant?

### Execution

Can the learner perform the skill without step-by-step prompting?

### Explanation

Can the learner explain why the method works and why it fits this case?

### Error detection

Can the learner catch an important error in their own work or diagnose a plausible wrong approach?

### Transfer

Can the learner apply the same underlying principle when the surface form changes?

### Delayed retrieval

For durable skills, can the learner still use the skill after time has passed without rereading the explanation first?

## Final mastery proof

A strong final test should resemble future reality:

- realistic information;
- no hidden scaffold from the teaching conversation;
- a fresh or meaningfully changed case;
- one or more plausible traps when appropriate;
- an observable pass/fail criterion defined before judging the result.

The key question is:

```text
Can the learner recognize and solve this when nobody tells them what lesson it belongs to?
```

## Transfer variation

Change one or more of:

- surface wording;
- context;
- irrelevant details;
- order of information;
- apparent similarity;
- competing method;
- missing information;
- plausible distractor.

Do not make transfer so different that it tests an unrelated prerequisite instead of the target skill.

## Mastery Evidence Record

For a meaningful skill in a longer arc, preserve why the current status is justified:

```text
SKILL:
STATUS: NEEDS_BASELINE | GUIDED | PRACTICING | TRANSFER_TEST | INDEPENDENT
CASE / TASK:
ASSISTANCE USED: highest material Assistance Ladder level
RECOGNITION: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
EXECUTION: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
EXPLANATION: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
ERROR DETECTION: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
TRANSFER: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
DELAYED RETRIEVAL: PASS | FAIL | NOT TESTED
VERDICT:
LIMITATIONS / NEXT PROOF:
```

Rules:

- guided success is practice evidence, not independent mastery evidence;
- if the teacher supplied the decisive reasoning, the case cannot prove independence;
- a correct answer for the wrong reason does not pass the relevant reasoning criterion;
- `NOT TESTED` is better than invented evidence;
- an `INDEPENDENT` status should point to the fresh case that justified it;
- later contradictory evidence may reopen a passed skill.

Not every small lesson needs a formal record. Use it when future teaching decisions will rely on the mastery claim.

## After a failed mastery test

Do not reset the learner to the beginning automatically.

Diagnose the failure:

- recognition failure;
- execution failure;
- explanation failure;
- monitoring failure;
- transfer failure;
- forgotten prerequisite;
- misleading or poorly designed test.

Return to the lowest Assistance Ladder rung that addresses the observed weakness, then test again with a different case.

## Stop condition

When the agreed mastery proof passes without material assistance, mark the skill independent and stop manufacturing practice merely to continue the process.
