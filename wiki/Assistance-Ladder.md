# The Assistance Ladder

The core Rung rule is:

> **Use the least help that allows productive progress, then return responsibility to the learner.**

This avoids two common failures:

- **over-helping:** the teacher does the reasoning and the learner merely follows;
- **under-helping:** the learner lacks the knowledge needed to reason and is left guessing.

The ladder moves from maximum learner independence to maximum teacher support.

## Level 0 — Independent attempt

> "Try it."

Use when the learner has enough knowledge to make a meaningful attempt.

The teacher observes rather than pre-correcting.

## Level 1 — Diagnostic question

Ask a reusable question tied to a known failure mode.

Examples:

- What evidence supports that?
- Who is actually making the decision here?
- Which condition in the problem tells you which formula applies?
- What would falsify your interpretation?

A strong diagnostic question should eventually become something the learner asks themselves.

## Level 2 — Attention cue

Point toward the relevant feature without supplying the interpretation.

> Compare what the character says she wants with what she actually chooses.

> Look specifically at the units in those two quantities.

The learner still performs the substantive reasoning.

## Level 3 — Recall cue

Ask the learner to retrieve a known principle.

> Which of our standing principles applies here?

> What was the test we used the last time this happened?

This combines problem solving with **retrieval practice**: the learner must produce the relevant rule rather than merely recognize it when shown. See [Research Foundations](Research-Foundations).

## Level 4 — Narrow the search space

Reduce complexity without deciding the answer.

> The failure is in either motive or consequence. Which one fails, and what evidence shows it?

Choices are allowed here only as scaffolding. The learner must justify the choice.

Rung therefore does **not** use an absolute rule that multiple-choice prompts are bad. The problem is using options as a substitute for reasoning.

## Level 5 — Explain the missing concept

If the learner lacks prerequisite knowledge, teach the concept directly and concisely.

Then immediately ask the learner to apply it.

```text
explain
→ learner restates
→ learner applies
```

This is where Rung differs from a rigid "questions only" model. A question cannot make a learner retrieve knowledge they have never acquired.

## Level 6 — Worked analogous example

Demonstrate the method on a **different** example.

Then return to the original or a parallel problem.

Worked-example research and cognitive-load theory show why stronger guidance can be especially useful for novices learning complex tasks. As knowledge grows, that guidance should fade. See Paas & van Merriënboer (2020) and the related sources on [Research Foundations](Research-Foundations).

## Level 7 — Partial solution to the current problem

Show only the part needed to restore productive work.

The learner completes the remaining reasoning.

Use when an analogous example is still too distant or the learner is blocked by one local step.

## Level 8 — Full solution

Use when continuing the current struggle has little educational value, or when safety, time, or consequence makes further trial-and-error inappropriate.

A full solution does **not** certify learning.

After showing it, require:

1. explanation in the learner's own words;
2. a similar problem without the solution visible;
3. a meaningfully changed problem requiring transfer.

## Guidance must fade

The assistance ladder is directional.

If the learner succeeds repeatedly with a Level 2 cue, do not keep giving Level 5 explanations.

Aim for:

```text
high structure
→ moderate structure
→ light cueing
→ independent work
→ audit only
```

This principle is compatible with research showing an **expertise-reversal effect**: instructional methods that help novices can become redundant or even detrimental for more knowledgeable learners. See Kalyuga et al. (2003) on [Research Foundations](Research-Foundations).

## Productive struggle test

Before withholding more help, ask whether the learner is still making interpretable progress.

### Productive struggle

The learner:

- has enough prerequisite knowledge to try;
- generates plausible approaches;
- makes mistakes that reveal reasoning;
- responds to targeted cues;
- can explain what they are attempting.

### Useless struggle

The learner:

- is guessing randomly;
- lacks required concepts;
- repeats the same move without understanding;
- cannot interpret feedback;
- is spending effort on mechanics unrelated to the target skill.

When struggle becomes useless, move **up** the assistance ladder.

Difficulty is not the objective. Learning is.
